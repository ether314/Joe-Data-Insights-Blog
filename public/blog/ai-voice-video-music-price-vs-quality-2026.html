Charted: AI Models Tied for First in Voice and Video Cost 7× to 12× Apart

Theta Scribe Research Desk·Published Oct 11, 2026·8 min read·Read the figure, then the sourced argument

How this analysis was made

Published by the Theta Scribe research desk. The charts on this page are original figures built for this story from public leaderboards and providers' own pricing pages, snapshot Oct 11, 2026 — not a scraped screenshot gallery. On blind-vote leaderboards, 7 video models and 7 voice models are statistically tied with the leader, at tracked prices 7× and 12× apart. Across 75 voice models, price barely predicts quality (rank correlation 0.12).

Pick the best-rated AI video generator on a blind-vote leaderboard and you will find it is not alone. Artificial Analysis' text-to-video arena ranks 29 models with native audio on social-media and creator content. The leader, Dreamina Seedance 2.5, scores 1188 Elo with a 95% interval of 1158 to 1218. Six other models have intervals that overlap it, so the votes cannot reliably separate them from the leader. Six of the seven carry a tracked price, and those prices run from $4.80 to $34.12 per minute of finished video: a 7.1× spread for results that voters could not tell apart.

Voice shows the same pattern, more sharply. On the speech arena's entertainment board, seven of 89 models tie for first, and all seven have a tracked price, from $0.0066 per minute (Simba 3.2) to $0.08 per minute (Eleven v4). That is a 12× spread. Across the 75 voice models that have both a price and an entertainment score, the rank correlation between price and quality is 0.12, close to no relationship. Video prices carry more signal (0.45 across 27 models), but the top of the board is crowded there too.

What the numbers are

Everything here comes from a database built on October 11, 2026. It converts every price to US dollars per minute and sets it beside published quality scores: 265 models, 639 price rows (366 read from providers' own pages, 232 of those subscription or credit plans, and 273 tracked by Artificial Analysis or LMArena) and 4,661 scores from 13 benchmarks. Quality means blind human preference: listeners or viewers pick the better of two outputs, and the votes become Elo-style scores with a 95% interval. Scores from different boards are not comparable, so each figure compares models inside a single board. “Statistically tied” below means a model's interval overlaps the leader's.

Video: seven models share the top, at prices 7× apart

Figure 1 (above) plots each priced model's social-media score against its tracked price per minute of video with native audio, on a log scale. The dashed line joins the best-value frontier: the models that no other model beats on both score and price. It has four members: Agnes-Video-2.5 ($1.50 per minute, 1089 Elo), MiniMax H3 at 768p ($4.80, 1155), Wan 3.0 ($12.00, 1169) and Seedance 2.5 ($34.12, 1188).

The top of that line is the expensive part. Moving from MiniMax H3 to Seedance 2.5 costs 7.1× as much for 33 more Elo points. At that gap the Elo formula implies Seedance 2.5 would win about 55% of head-to-head votes against MiniMax H3, so a real edge, but a small one, and the two intervals overlap. Wan 3.0 sits in between: 19 points below the leader at 35% of its tracked price.

Two of these tracked prices disagree with the provider's own page. Seedance 2.5 is tracked at $34.12 per minute while BytePlus lists $18.18 at 720p; at the lower figure the premium over MiniMax H3 shrinks from 7.1× to 3.8×. MiniMax H3 Max is tracked at $4.80 and MiniMax's page lists $3.00 at 480p. Confirm the resolution you need before budgeting.

Voice: price barely predicts what listeners prefer

Figure 2 repeats the exercise for speech. The seven models tied with the leader are Simba 3.2 ($0.0066 per minute), Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS ($0.0165), Qwen-Audio-3.1-TTS-Plus and Qwen-Audio-3.0-TTS-Plus ($0.0193 each), Eleven v4 Turbo ($0.04), Sonic 3.6 ($0.049) and Eleven v4 ($0.08). The cleanest example sits inside one product family. Eleven v4 and Eleven v4 Turbo score 1287 and 1286 on the entertainment board, 1308 and 1310 on knowledge sharing, and 1323 and 1327 overall: the same quality within noise. The standard model is tracked at $0.08 per minute and the Turbo variant at $0.04, and ElevenLabs' pricing page carries the same two figures.

Listener preference is not the only thing worth testing. Artificial Analysis also runs a pronunciation test in which reviewers judge whether hard items such as numbers, codes and brand names are said correctly. Among the tied models that have a pass rate, scores range from 74.5% to 91.7%, and price does not order them: Sonic 3.6, the second most expensive, has the lowest.

Simba 3.2 ties the leader on listener preference at $0.0066 per minute but passes 77.6% of pronunciation checks, against 90.1% for Eleven v4 Turbo at about six times the price. The two measures agree only moderately (rank correlation 0.67 across 28 shared models), so a cheap voice that ties on taste can still miss more hard items in a script full of numbers. The best trade in the table is Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS: 89.5% at the tracked $0.0165, within a point of Eleven v4 Turbo at under half its price.

Voice prices also move. Google lists Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS at $9 per million audio tokens through December 31, 2026 and $18 from January 1, 2027. At 25 audio tokens per second that is about $0.0135 per minute now and $0.027 after the increase, against the $0.0165 tracked.

Music: the leader is also the cheapest, among the four with a price

Artificial Analysis runs two music arenas, one for instrumentals and one for tracks with vocals, covering 19 models. The arenas publish no prices, so every music price here comes from providers' own pages, and only four models could be priced. Most music tools sell credit plans rather than per-minute API rates, so a music price is the cheapest effective cost per minute across a provider's paid plans and API at full use of the allowance, free tiers excluded. A song is assumed to run three minutes.

Of the four priced models, Suno v6 leads both boards (1140 instrumental, 1142 with vocals) at about $0.004 per minute, or $0.012 per three-minute song. Google's Lyria 3 Pro and Lyria 3.5 follow at 1078 and 1076 on the instrumental board and $0.08 per song through the API. Eleven Music scores 1021 at about $0.15 per minute ($0.446 per song). The other 15 models have no price here. That includes Mureka V9 and V9.5, which score between Suno and Lyria on the instrumental board. Their pages were blocked or script-only, or list no price, and MiniMax's paid music API is listed as discontinued. Commercial-use terms vary by plan, so check them before posting generated music on social platforms.

How far to trust the prices

For 53 models the database holds both a tracked price and the provider's own. For 34 of them the two agree within 8%. For 19 they do not. Figure 4 shows the eight widest gaps as the provider's price divided by the tracked price. Provider pages are cheaper than the tracked price in 6 of those eight, and in 16 of all 19 disagreements, so budget from the provider's page for the exact resolution and tier you plan to use.

Do the benchmarks agree?

Rank correlation measures how similarly two boards order the models they share (1 means the same order). The video boards and Artificial Analysis' own slices agree closely, which is why a slice mostly re-ranks the overall order. The voice boards agree less, and the objective pronunciation test only partly follows preference.

Caveats and confidence

  • Elo is relative. Scores are comparable only inside one board. Each figure here uses one board.
  • “Tied” is a permissive test. Overlap with the leader's 95% interval says the votes cannot separate a model from the leader, not that the two are equal.
  • Arenas measure typical prompts and raters, not your brand voice or niche. Re-test a shortlist on your own scripts.
  • Tracked prices are list prices at default settings. Real cost depends on resolution, audio, plan and unused credits. Figure 4 shows where a provider's page disagrees.
  • Music is thin. Four of 19 models are priced. Suno's price assumes a three-minute song and full use of a plan's credits, and unused credits raise it.
  • Missing is not zero. Providers whose pages could not be read are absent from the price comparison. A $0 price, such as the one on MiMo-V2.5-TTS, is a promotion with no end date.
  • Vintages differ. Artificial Analysis and Hugging Face data were captured on October 11, 2026; LMArena's vote cutoff is September 21, 2026. Prices change.

Bottom line for desks

In these arenas, a price is a weak guide to quality: nearly none in voice (0.12), modest in video (0.45). A better rule is to shortlist by the leader's interval, not the leader. Anything whose interval overlaps it is a candidate, and the cheapest candidate that passes your own test is the one to buy. On this snapshot that means tracked video prices from $4.80 per minute against $34.12, and voices from $0.0066 per minute against $0.08, subject to the pronunciation check for voice, the provider-page check for video, and a listen on your own material.
